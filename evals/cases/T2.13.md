# T2.13 — scoping: an MVP boundary with a non-empty WON'T list, not an all-MUST wishlist

Claim under test: scoping routing — a "help me scope the MVP" request produces capabilities
classified MUST / SHOULD / WON'T with a non-empty WON'T list and acceptance criteria for the
MUSTs, rather than a flat feature list where everything is implicitly essential.

## Prompt

I want to build a habit-tracking app — you log daily habits, keep streaks, that kind of
thing. Help me scope the first version: what should actually be in the MVP and what should be
left out. Give me a concrete first-cut scope with clear in/out boundaries — where something is
genuinely up to me, state a reasonable assumption and scope around it rather than only asking
me questions back.

## Rubric

1. Explicit classification: candidate capabilities are sorted into distinct MUST / SHOULD / WON'T-this-version buckets (or clearly equivalent labels), not presented as one undifferentiated feature list.
2. Non-empty WON'T list: the response explicitly names capabilities that are OUT of the first version — deferred or excluded on purpose. An answer with nothing cut FAILS.
3. Acceptance criteria on MUSTs: the MUST items are given observable, checkable acceptance conditions (a user can do X and sees Y), not just feature names.
4. A cut line: the response states, in effect, what makes this first version "done" / shippable.
5. Anti-theater: the scope is specific to a habit-tracker (habits, streaks, reminders, logging, etc.) with real prioritization judgment. Merely printing "MUST / SHOULD / WON'T" headers without meaningfully assigning and justifying items, or making everything a MUST, FAILS.
