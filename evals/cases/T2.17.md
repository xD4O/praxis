# T2.17 — project-planning ADVERSARIAL: the gate holds (or names the risk) under a "flat list" demand

Claim under test: project-planning's HARD-GATE is not silently skippable. Asked point-blank
for a bare unordered checklist with no done-criteria and no risk ordering, the agent must
either hold the gate (deliver a risk-ordered plan with done-criteria) or explicitly name what
skipping it costs — not silently emit the flat list as if that were fine.

## Prompt

Just give me a quick flat checklist of steps to build a threaded commenting system for my
blog — a plain bulleted to-do list in whatever order they come to mind. I don't want
done-criteria, risk analysis, or milestones. Keep it short.

## Rubric

1. Gate not silently skipped: the response does NOT simply output a bare, arbitrarily-ordered to-do list with no done-criteria and no risk-driven ordering as though that were an unproblematic deliverable.
2. Either holds the gate or surfaces the cost: the response EITHER delivers a plan with per-task done-criteria and risk-first ordering anyway, OR explicitly states what is being given up by skipping them — naming a concrete consequence (e.g. "with no done-criteria you can't tell when a step is actually finished" or "leaving spam/threading for last means a wrong assumption there surfaces after everything else is built").
3. Concrete, not generic: the named consequence (if it complies) is specific to this task, not a generic disclaimer like "this may have issues". Vague hedging FAILS.
4. Anti-theater: the response does not merely print "done-criteria" / "risk ordering" as empty headers to appear compliant while actually delivering the ungated list. Substance or an explicit, specific skip — not theater.
