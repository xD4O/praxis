# T2.14 — domain-modeling: invariants and unrepresentable illegal states, not a bag of tables

Claim under test: domain-modeling routing — a "design the schema" request produces entities
with stated invariants, relationships with named lifecycle ownership, and an explicit check
that domain-forbidden states are made unrepresentable — not just a list of tables and columns.

## Prompt

Design the data model for a library book-lending system. Members borrow books; a title can
have multiple physical copies; a loan has a due date; and members can place a hold on a title
when all copies are out. The stack is Postgres — give me the core entities and relationships.
Where a detail is unspecified, state a reasonable assumption and model it rather than stopping
to ask.

## Rubric

1. Entities with invariants: each core entity (member, title, copy, loan, hold, or equivalent) is given at least one always-true rule, not just a field list. A model that lists columns with no invariants FAILS.
2. Ownership and cardinality: relationships state cardinality and which side owns the lifecycle (e.g. a copy belongs to a title; a loan references a copy and a member), not just that a foreign key exists.
3. Forbidden states addressed: the response identifies at least one state the domain forbids — plausibly "one physical copy on loan to two members at once", or "a hold placed when copies are actually available" — and shows how the model prevents it (unique constraint, required field, structure) or flags it as runtime-guarded with a reason.
4. Domain language: entities use library-domain vocabulary (title vs copy distinction, loan, hold, due date) rather than invented generic names.
5. Anti-theater: the invariants and forbidden-state prevention are specific and correct for THIS domain. Naming "invariants" and "illegal states" as headings without concrete, domain-correct content FAILS.
